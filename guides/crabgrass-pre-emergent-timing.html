When to put down crabgrass pre-emergent

Soil temperature, not the date on the calendar.

The rule

Crabgrass starts germinating once the soil at about 2 inches deep holds around 55°F for several days in a row. A pre-emergent works by forming a barrier in the top of the soil that stops seedlings as they sprout — so it has to be down and watered in before that happens. It doesn't kill crabgrass that's already up.

A handy backyard signal: many extension services use forsythia bloom as the cue. When the forsythia is in full yellow bloom, it's time.

Rough timing by region

These are typical windows — your soil temperature is the real trigger, and it moves with the weather each year.

RegionTypical window
Florida & Gulf CoastLate January – February
Warm humid South (GA, AL, MS, the Carolinas' coastal plain)February – early March
Transition zone (Kansas City → St. Louis → Louisville → DC)Mid-March – early April
Cool humid North (Ohio Valley north, Mid-Atlantic north, New England)April
Northern tier & mountain WestLate April – May

Split it in two

Serious lawn people split the yearly rate into two applications: the first at the start of the window, the second about 6–8 weeks later. That stretches the barrier through the long, warm end of spring when late crabgrass sneaks in. Always stay within the product label's yearly maximum.

Water it in

A granular or sprayed pre-emergent needs water to move it into the soil — usually about half an inch of rain or irrigation within a few days (check the label). Left sitting on top, it breaks down.

The mistakes that waste a bag

  • Seeding after a pre-emergent. Most pre-emergents (prodiamine, dithiopyr, pendimethalin) stop grass seed too — often for months. If you're seeding this spring, use a seeding-safe option like mesotrione (Tenacity) instead, or plan to seed in fall.
  • Core aerating after application. Punching holes breaks the barrier. Aerate before, or wait.
  • Going too late. If crabgrass is already up, a straight pre-emergent won't touch it. Dithiopyr has some early post-emergent activity on very young crabgrass; beyond that you need a post-emergent.
  • Warm-season lawns and Poa annua. In the South, a second pre-emergent in early fall stops Poa annua (annual bluegrass) — the winter weed that ruins dormant bermuda.

Always follow the label. The label is the law for rates, timing, grass safety and re-entry. Some products aren't registered in every state — check before you buy.
